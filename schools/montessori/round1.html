Montessori, plainly

Montessori is a way of teaching that began in Rome in 1907 with Dr. Maria Montessori, a physician. Children work with real materials, choose their work within clear limits, and move at their own pace in mixed-age classrooms, with a trained teacher who watches and guides each child.

Isn't it just play?

It is work the child chooses. Every material teaches one thing on purpose, and it shows the child their own mistakes: a cylinder that doesn't fit, a tower that leans. That's how children learn to correct themselves without waiting for an adult.

Can children do whatever they want?

Freedom within limits. Children choose from work they have been shown, finish what they start and put it back on the shelf for the next child. The teacher keeps a record of every child's work and steps in when needed.

Why are ages mixed?

Each class spans three years. Younger children learn by watching older ones, and older children learn by helping. Your child keeps the same teacher for three years, so the teacher knows them well.

Will my child be ready for another school later?

We track the same reading and math skills public schools expect at each grade, and we show you where your child stands at every conference. Families who move on take a full written report with them.

Is it right for my child?

Busy children, quiet children, children who need more time and children who race ahead all find their place here. The best way to know is to watch a real morning. Come observe a class.

Is it religious?

No. Fernhill is independent and nonsectarian. Families of every faith, and of none, are welcome.

Is it academic enough?

Yes. Children get there by understanding first, with their hands, then on paper. This is the path most children take. Ages are typical, not deadlines: each child moves at their own pace.

Reading and writing

  1. 3–4Sandpaper lettersLetter sounds learned by touch
  2. 4–5Moveable alphabetBuilding words before the hand can write them
  3. 5–6First booksReading short books, in English and Spanish
  4. 6–9Grammar symbolsHow sentences work, word by word
  5. 9–12Research reportsQuestions they choose, written up and presented
  6. 12–14Essays and seminarsArguing a point in writing and out loud

By the end of Children's House, many children read short books and add numbers into the thousands with the beads. We show you exactly where your child is at each conference, in plain words, with the work in front of you.
