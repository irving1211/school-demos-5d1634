Four years and after

Yaritza's twelve years with us, told the way we would tell it at supper. She is a composite; names and photographs are samples.

Grade 5, September

The first morning starts at 7:30

Breakfast comes first, at long tables, before anyone opens a book. Then reading, writing and math in classes of sixteen (sample). Each student gets an advisor, and the advisor calls home every Friday, in English or Spanish.

Grade 6, the long day

Homework happens here, not at the kitchen table

At 3:45 the day does not end. Study hall runs until 4:45 with the same teachers who taught the lesson. Then a hot supper, and home by 5:15.

4:11

A question asked in study hall at 4:10 gets an answer at 4:11, from the teacher who taught the lesson.

  • 3:45Study hall with the teachers
  • 4:45Supper together
  • 5:00Pickup, or walk home with a group

Grade 7, July

Summer is part of the school year

Five weeks every July: reading in the mornings, swimming lessons in the afternoons, and one week at a lake camp in New Hampshire (sample). For many students it is the first time away from home. It is free, like everything else.

Grade 8, next steps

Choosing a high school, together

Algebra by eighth grade. In the fall we visit high schools with every family and help with each application and scholarship form. In the winter, students practice interviews with volunteers until the questions feel ordinary.

After, eight more years

Graduation is where the second part starts

Our graduate-support director keeps a list of every alum and calls each one every month, through high school and through college.

The calls are about ordinary things: a grade that slipped, a financial-aid form due Friday, a ride to the first campus visit. When something needs more than a call, a tutor or a volunteer mentor steps in.
