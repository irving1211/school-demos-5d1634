montessori, plainly

A way of teaching that began in Rome in 1907 with Dr. Maria Montessori, a physician. Here is what it means for your child, one question at a time.

Isn’t it just play?
It is work the child chooses. Every material teaches one thing on purpose, and it shows the child their own mistakes: a cylinder that doesn’t fit, a tower that leans. Children learn to correct themselves without waiting for an adult.
Can children do whatever they want?
Freedom within limits. Children choose from work they have been shown, finish what they start and put it back on the shelf for the next child. The teacher keeps a record of every child’s work and steps in when needed.
Why are ages mixed?
Each class spans three years. Younger children learn by watching older ones, and older children learn by helping. Your child keeps the same teacher for three years.
Is it academic enough?
Yes. Children understand with their hands first, then do the same steps on paper. The table below shows the path most children take.
Will my child be ready for another school later?
We track the same reading and math skills public schools expect at each grade, and we show you where your child stands at every conference. Families who move on take a full written report with them.
Is it religious?
No. Fernhill is independent and nonsectarian. Families of every faith, and of none, are welcome.

The path most children take

Ages are typical, not deadlines: each child moves at their own pace.

3–6Children’s HouseGolden beads and the stamp game: place value, then adding and subtracting into the thousands.Sandpaper letters and the moveable alphabet, then first books in English and Spanish.
6–9Lower ElementaryThe bead stair and bead chains: math facts, skip counting, squares and cubes.Grammar symbols and sentence analysis; first written reports.
9–12Upper ElementaryThe checkerboard and fraction insets: long multiplication and fractions, then the same steps on paper.Research questions they choose, written up and presented to the class.
12–14AdolescentPre-algebra and algebra, ready for high school.Essays and seminars: arguing a point in writing and out loud.
